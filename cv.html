Engineering & Data Projects

Municipal Water Infrastructure - Project Controls System

2026
Developed a project-controls and risk-management system for a simulated $96M municipal water capital program spanning 12 infrastructure projects over a 36-month planning horizon. The purpose of the project was to model how an infrastructure program could be monitored across scope, schedule, budget, risk, milestones, contractors, and contingency using industrial engineering and project-management methods. Built a work breakdown structure, 428-activity baseline schedule, dependency network, cost-loaded model, earned-value calculations, risk and change tracking, and executive-level reporting. Stress-tested the model using a $600K contractor change and six-week schedule delay to evaluate effects on forecast completion, downstream milestones, contingency, and resource allocation. The final system provided a unified view of program performance and demonstrated how project controls can be used to identify projects requiring intervention and support management decisions.

NYC 311 Service Requests - Lakehouse Pipeline

2026
Built an end-to-end data engineering pipeline to transform raw NYC 311 service-request data into a repeatable analytics environment for studying municipal service activity. The goal was to move beyond one-time CSV analysis and create a scalable workflow that could continually ingest, clean, validate, and organize service-request records for downstream analysis. Developed API ingestion and incremental ETL workflows in Python, stored processed data in Parquet, modeled analytics-ready tables, and used DuckDB for fast local querying and validation. The completed pipeline created a reproducible lakehouse-style workflow that reduced the amount of manual preparation required before analyzing complaint types, agencies, geography, and service patterns.

Gear Fault Diagnosis - Machine Learning Classification

2022
Implemented a machine-learning approach for diagnosing mechanical gear faults from vibration signals, based on published research by Zhou and Tang. The project focused on the manufacturing problem of identifying fault conditions when only limited labeled examples are available, where conventional supervised models can struggle to generalize. Used kernel principal component analysis to reduce high-dimensional vibration features and a fuzzy neural network to classify gear-condition patterns. The resulting workflow demonstrated how dimensionality reduction and fuzzy classification can be combined to distinguish mechanical fault states while working with constrained training data, providing a practical predictive-maintenance application of machine learning.

MATCH-Conduct Treatment Outcomes - Research Analysis

2025-2026
Co-authored research examining why youth receiving the MATCH-Conduct behavioral-health protocol differed in treatment completion and symptom outcomes across Connecticut's statewide provider network. Analyzed 788 discharged cases using logistic and linear regression to evaluate demographic, clinical, and educational predictors of treatment success. Higher caregiver-reported symptom severity and having an IEP at intake were associated with lower odds of successful treatment completion, while higher initial severity and co-occurring depression were associated with greater symptom severity at discharge. Outcomes did not significantly differ by age, sex, race, or ethnicity. The findings highlighted a potential need for greater school coordination and additional support for youth with IEPs while also suggesting comparable MATCH-C outcomes across demographic groups.
