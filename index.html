Industrial Engineering / Data Analytics Engineering

My interest in data, systems, and human behavior started with a simple question: how do people make decisions, work together, and move through complex environments? At the University of Connecticut, I studied psychological sciences, where I became interested in motivation, learning, social coordination, and the ways people interact with the systems around them. That background taught me to look beyond the numbers and ask what the data says about real people, real processes, and real outcomes.

Over time, my work in research and healthcare quality improvement pushed me toward data analytics, engineering, and systems thinking. As a Data Analyst Fellow at the Child Health and Development Institute, I worked with large behavioral health datasets, cleaned and transformed messy data, built reporting tools, and supported quality improvement efforts across statewide service systems. That experience helped me realize that I enjoy turning complex information into something organized, useful, and actionable.

At Northeastern, I am building on that foundation through graduate study in industrial engineering and data analytics. I am especially interested in product analytics, operations, live service systems, process improvement, and data engineering. I enjoy work that combines technical problem-solving with human-centered thinking, whether that means improving a dashboard, studying user behavior, optimizing a workflow, or helping teams make better decisions from data.

Outside of academics and work, I enjoy reading, training, hiking, playing guitar, and exploring games and sports from both a player and systems perspective. I am drawn to spaces where creativity and structure meet, and I hope to build a career that lets me use data, design, and systems thinking to improve products, services, and human experiences.
