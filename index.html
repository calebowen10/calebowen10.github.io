About me

People, data, and systems. That’s where my work begins.

I began in psychology at the University of Connecticut, where questions about human behavior, decisions, and interaction became the foundation of my work.

As a Data Analyst Fellow at the Child Health and Development Institute, I worked on quality improvement and reporting across behavioral health services. That experience strengthened my interest in how information becomes real operational change.

Now, as a graduate student in Industrial Engineering at Northeastern University, I bring these perspectives together. I’m especially drawn to operations, product experiences, data systems, healthcare, and the games and entertainment industry.

The why behind my work

Motivation

It isn't just about what the data says. It's what we can do with it.

I’m interested in systems that touch everyday life: access to healthcare, the tools people use, the processes behind great products, and the experiences that make technology meaningful.

My approach combines an understanding of human behavior with analytical rigor—asking not only whether a system works, but who it works for, where it breaks down, and how it could improve.
