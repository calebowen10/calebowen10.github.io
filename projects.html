Industrial Engineering & Project Controls

Municipal Water Infrastructure Capital Improvement Program

Project Controls, Earned Value, Cost Forecasting & Schedule Analysis
Completed Portfolio Case Study · 2026

I developed this project to simulate the management environment behind a large municipal infrastructure capital program. The fictional Harborview Water & Sewer Department is responsible for delivering 12 water infrastructure projects within a $96.0 million approved program envelope. Of that amount, $88.5 million is assigned to baselined project work and $7.5 million is retained as program contingency.

The goal was not to perform civil engineering design. Instead, I wanted to model the industrial engineering and project-controls problem behind infrastructure delivery: how program leadership can monitor scope, schedule, cost, risk, procurement, contractors, milestones, resources, changes, and forecast performance across multiple projects at once.

I developed the program's work breakdown structure, cost architecture, control accounts, work packages, and a 428-activity baseline schedule. I then connected planned schedule performance to a cost-loaded Earned Value Management framework capable of tracking Planned Value (PV), Earned Value (EV), Actual Cost (AC), Cost Performance Index (CPI), Schedule Performance Index (SPI), Estimate at Completion (EAC), and Variance at Completion (VAC).

Program Definition → WBS → Baseline Schedule → Cost Model → Earned Value → Forecasting → Scenario Analysis → Executive Decision Support
$96.0M Approved Program Envelope
12 Capital Projects
428 Baseline Schedule Activities
$7.5M Program Contingency

At the March 31, 2026 program status point, planned work had reached approximately $54.44 million, while earned value was approximately $53.05 million. Actual cost had reached approximately $57.70 million. The resulting program CPI of approximately 0.92 showed considerably more cost pressure than schedule pressure, while an SPI of approximately 0.97 indicated that the program remained relatively close to its planned schedule position.

Project-level forecasts produced an aggregate EAC of approximately $95.43 million. Although this exceeds the $88.5 million baselined project BAC, it remains just below the original $96 million total program funding envelope, meaning most of the original contingency would be consumed under the modeled forecast.

Selected Findings

  • P05 showed the weakest modeled cost efficiency, with a CPI of approximately 0.86.
  • P08 showed the greatest schedule pressure, with an SPI of approximately 0.80.
  • P03, the treatment plant upgrade, represented the largest individual forecast exposure, with an EAC of roughly $22.49M against a $20.30M BAC.
  • The overall program remained much closer to its schedule baseline than to its cost baseline, making cost control the more immediate portfolio-level management issue.

I also incorporated schedule-recovery assumptions, commitment tracking, procurement exposure, and project-specific scenario assumptions. This allowed the model to move beyond historical reporting and instead answer management questions such as: Which projects require intervention? Which projects are driving forecast variance? How much contingency remains? Where are schedule risks concentrated? And which current commitments have not yet been incurred as actual cost?

Machine Learning & Manufacturing

Gear Fault Diagnosis Using Kernel PCA & Fuzzy Neural Networks

Vibration-Based Fault Classification & Predictive Maintenance
Engineering Research Project · 2022

This project examined how machine-learning methods can support mechanical fault diagnosis when only limited labeled examples of equipment failure are available. The problem is particularly relevant to predictive maintenance because real mechanical failures are often uncommon, expensive to reproduce, or unsafe to deliberately generate simply to create training data.

Inspired by published work from Zhou and Tang, I implemented a fault-diagnosis workflow that combines Kernel Principal Component Analysis (KPCA) with a Fuzzy Neural Network (FNN). The project used vibration information as the basis for distinguishing different gearbox operating and fault conditions.

Vibration Data → Feature Representation → Kernel PCA → Fuzzy Membership Functions → Neural Network → Fault Classification

KPCA was used to transform nonlinear and correlated vibration information into a lower-dimensional feature space. This reduced the amount of information passed into the classifier while preserving the relationships most useful for separating operating conditions.

The reduced feature representation was then passed into a fuzzy neural network. Rather than forcing every observation directly into a hard class boundary, the fuzzy architecture represents the degree to which an observation belongs to different learned fault patterns. This is valuable for machinery diagnosis because mechanical faults do not always produce perfectly distinct vibration signatures.

The project gave me experience translating an academic engineering methodology into a complete analytical workflow that included feature processing, nonlinear dimensionality reduction, fuzzy reasoning, classification, and model interpretation. More broadly, it introduced me to the challenge of designing machine-learning systems for engineering environments where labeled data are much more constrained than they typically are in conventional analytics.

Data Engineering

NYC 311 Service Requests - Lakehouse Pipeline

API Ingestion, Incremental ETL, Parquet & DuckDB
Data Engineering Project · 2026

I built this project around NYC 311 service-request data to practice designing a repeatable analytical data system rather than performing a one-time notebook analysis. NYC 311 produces a large, continuously changing stream of operational records across agencies, complaint types, neighborhoods, and service categories.

The objective was to create a pipeline that separated ingestion, cleaning, transformation, storage, validation, and analytics. Python workflows ingest records from the NYC Open Data API, standardize fields and types, process new records incrementally, and create structured analytical datasets.

NYC Open Data API → Python Ingestion → Validation → Incremental ETL → Parquet Storage → DuckDB → Analytics-Ready Data

Processed records were stored in Parquet to provide compact columnar storage, while DuckDB provided a lightweight analytical query engine without requiring a separate database server. Data-quality checks were incorporated to identify missing IDs, malformed dates, duplicate records, inconsistent categories, and other issues that could affect downstream analysis.

The result is a reproducible lakehouse-style workflow that can be rerun as additional service requests are added. Instead of repeatedly cleaning raw files before every analysis, the pipeline creates reusable analytical data that can support investigation of complaint volume, geography, agency workload, service categories, and changes in municipal demand over time.

Healthcare Analytics & Research

MATCH-Conduct Treatment Outcomes

Statewide Behavioral Health Outcomes Analysis
Co-Authored Research · 2025–2026

This research examined treatment completion and symptom outcomes among children and adolescents receiving the Conduct component of the Modular Approach to Therapy for Children with Anxiety, Depression, Trauma, or Conduct Problems (MATCH-ADTC) across Connecticut's statewide evidence-based treatment network.

The analysis included 788 children and adolescents ages 3–17 whose primary MATCH protocol was Conduct. Logistic regression was used to examine factors associated with successful treatment completion, while linear regression was used to examine symptom severity at discharge.

788 Youth Cases
22 Community Agencies
3–17 Participant Age Range
2 Models Logistic & Linear Regression

Selected Findings

  • Greater caregiver-reported symptom severity at intake was associated with lower odds of successful treatment completion.
  • Youth with an Individualized Education Program (IEP) also showed lower odds of successful treatment completion.
  • Greater initial symptom severity and co-occurring depression were associated with higher symptom severity at discharge.
  • Treatment outcomes did not significantly differ by age, sex, race, or ethnicity in the analyzed sample.

The findings suggest that clinical complexity and educational support needs may help identify youth who require additional coordination during treatment. The project also strengthened my experience working at the intersection of data analysis, implementation science, healthcare quality, and real-world service systems.

Professional Analytics Work

Statewide Behavioral Health Data & Quality Improvement

Child Health & Development Institute
Professional Experience

My professional analytics work at the Child Health & Development Institute centered on improving the quality and usefulness of behavioral-health data submitted across a statewide provider network. I worked with information from 18 provider organizations, each of which used different source structures, coding conventions, and reporting practices.

I developed validation, cleaning, standardization, and reporting workflows that improved usable data completeness from approximately 78% to 94% while reducing correction cycles and shortening recurring reporting timelines. I also supported analysis and reporting across tens of thousands of service records and dozens of operational indicators.

This work shaped how I approach technical projects today: accurate analysis depends on the systems surrounding the data. Data definitions, process design, validation, documentation, stakeholder communication, and implementation all matter as much as the statistical or technical method itself.
